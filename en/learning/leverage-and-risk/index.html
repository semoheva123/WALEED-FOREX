Margin and leverage: how exposure and risk can grow

Leverage allows a larger notional exposure relative to the amount set aside as margin. It does not reduce potential loss; it can make the effect of price changes on account equity faster and larger.

VALID VX editorial content · Last updated: 25 October 2026

Key termsIllustrationRisksChecklistReferences

1. Four key concepts

  • Margin: collateral a provider requires to open or maintain exposure, under the product rules.
  • Leverage: the ratio of notional exposure to allocated capital; practical limits and calculations vary by product and entity.
  • Free margin: funds a provider calculates as available to support existing positions or open additional ones.
  • Close-out or liquidation level: a contractual threshold that may trigger position closure. Do not assume closure occurs at a particular price or without additional loss.

2. A simplified arithmetic illustration—not a broker quote

For mathematical illustration only, if notional exposure is 10,000 currency units and required margin is 500 units, exposure relative to margin is 20 to 1. This does not mean maximum loss is fixed at 500: profit or loss changes with price, costs and close-out terms, and the outcome may exceed expectations.

Leverage describes exposure; it is not free funding and does not guarantee that a stop order will execute at its requested price. Spreads, slippage and gaps can change the result.

3. Risk factors to assess

  • Adverse market movements and rapid price changes.
  • Increased exposure from leverage or multiple correlated positions.
  • Spread widening, overnight financing, commissions and currency conversion.
  • Slippage, gaps and difficulty executing or closing during volatile conditions.
  • Differences in negative-balance protection and close-out rules across countries and entities.

Risk management does not eliminate risk. A demo account can help with platform familiarity, but may not reproduce live execution or the psychological effects of real-money trading.

4. Checklist before using a margined product

  1. Identify the legal entity that will contract with you, not only the brand name.
  2. Read the risk disclosure, initial and maintenance margin terms, and close-out procedure.
  3. Check whether a shortfall could exceed your balance and whether any protection applies to you.
  4. Compare the total cost, including spread, commission, financing and conversion.
  5. Verify the firm in the relevant regulator’s official register.
  6. Do not deposit money needed for living costs or rely on forecasts or historical returns.
